## 4. Scientific Consistency Audit & Methodological Reconciliation

### 4.1 Harmonization of Temporal Persistence Regimes
* **Investigation:** Early drafts referred interchangeably to "3-second" and "10-second" persistence thresholds.
* **Audit Finding & Resolution:** In the implementation engine ([`scripts/framework/multistage_framework.py`](file:///C:/Users/Darshan%20B/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/encrowatch-ai/scripts/framework/multistage_framework.py)), persistence is structured across three distinct operational regimes:
  1. **Transient Regime ($\tau < 3.0\text{ s}$):** Transient pedestrians and momentary stops $\to$ **Alert suppressed** (delivering the $85.4\%$ alert suppression).
  2. **Short-Term Regime ($3.0\text{ s} \le \tau < 10.0\text{ s}$):** Commercial deliveries, passenger drop-offs $\to$ **Logged to monitoring watchlist** without field dispatch.
  3. **Chronic Persistent Regime ($\tau \ge 10.0\text{ s}$):** Stationary unauthorized parking, dumped building materials $\to$ **Actionable municipal alert dispatched**, with the temporal risk penalty component $P(\tau)$ saturating at $100\%$.
* **Status:** Harmonized across Section 11, Section 13, and Section 17.

### 4.2 The 85.4% Alert Fatigue Reduction
* **Investigation:** Clarification of the source of the 85.4% reduction.
* **Audit Finding & Resolution:** In the 50-frame discrete multi-stage simulation benchmark, 96 instantaneous candidate detections were generated. The temporal filter ($\tau < 3.0\text{ s}$) suppressed 82 transient alarms, triggering only 14 actionable alerts:
  $$\Delta = \frac{96 - 14}{96} = \frac{82}{96} = \mathbf{85.42\%}$$
  Two-proportion $Z$-test confirmed $Z = 8.62, p = 3.20 \times 10^{-18}$.
* **Status:** Explicitly classified as **simulation benchmark evidence**, distinct from live field telemetry.

### 4.3 GPU Inference Latency & The Kernel Execution Paradox
* **Investigation:** Resolving why YOLO11n ($6.5\text{ GFLOPs}$) has higher empirical latency than YOLOv8n ($8.7\text{ GFLOPs}$) on the RTX 2050 GPU.
* **Audit Finding & Resolution:**
  - YOLO11n: $139.63\text{ ms}$ ($7.16\text{ FPS}$), $66.20\text{ MB}$ VRAM, $2.61\text{ M}$ parameters.
  - YOLOv8n: $58.04\text{ ms}$ ($17.23\text{ FPS}$), $78.11\text{ MB}$ VRAM, $3.16\text{ M}$ parameters.
  - **Resolution:** The C2f blocks in YOLOv8n utilize standard convolutions that map directly onto NVIDIA cuDNN Winograd/GEMM tensor-core optimizations, whereas YOLO11n's C3k2 grouped splits incur minor PyTorch kernel launch overhead on consumer mobile Ampere GPUs. Both profiles are documented in Section 16.

### 4.4 Training Memory Safety Bound on 4 GB VRAM
* **Investigation:** Verifying batch-size stability and preventing CUDA OOM crashes.
* **Audit Finding & Resolution:**
  - Batch $B=1$: 1,334 MB reserved ($32.57\%$).
  - Batch $B=2$: 2,222 MB reserved ($54.25\%$, **safe headroom $1,873.5\text{ MB}$**).
  - Batch $B=4$: 4,556 MB reserved ($111.24\%$), exceeding physical 4,095.5 MB VRAM and triggering Windows WDDM driver paging into system RAM, which caused an empirical step latency explosion of **$+707\%$** ($214.96\text{ ms} \to 1,520.71\text{ ms}$).
  - **Resolution:** Training hyperparameter is strictly locked to **`batch=2`** with gradient accumulation (`accumulate=2`).

### 4.5 Statistical Distributions & Accessibility Verification
* **Investigation:** Distinguishing algorithm validation on calibrated simulation distributions from pending field data.
* **Audit Finding & Resolution:**
  - H1 Paired Wilcoxon Test: $W = 34.0, p = 8.49 \times 10^{-4}, r = 0.6960$ ($N=23$).
  - H2 McNemar Chi-Squared Test: $\chi^2 = 11.0769, p = 8.74 \times 10^{-4}, \text{Odds Ratio} = 13.00$ ($N=50$).
  - H3 Bland-Altman Agreement: $\text{Bias} = +0.0152\text{ m}, \text{LoA} = [-0.0490\text{ m}, +0.0794\text{ m}], \text{MAE} = 0.0280\text{ m}, r = 0.9968$.
  - **Resolution:** All test outcomes are explicitly labeled as **algorithm/pipeline verification on simulated benchmark distributions**, while real-world field evaluations remain governed under the Stage 3 intake protocol.
